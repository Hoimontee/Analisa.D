Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD)

Average structure

When X-rays or neutrons interact with a crystalline powder, the periodic arrangement of atoms produces a powder diffraction pattern containing Bragg reflections and, depending on the material and experiment, diffuse-scattering contributions. Peak positions support phase identification, while quantitative phase analysis uses full-pattern intensities and appropriate models to estimate phase fractions. Rietveld refinement can refine lattice parameters and, where the data quality and structural model support it, parameters such as atomic positions, occupancies and displacement parameters. Powder diffraction is widely used for phase identification, quantitative phase analysis and crystallographic refinement of crystalline materials.

Total scattering & PDF

Local structure

Total-scattering measurements contain both Bragg and diffuse-scattering contributions. Rietveld refinement primarily models the Bragg component to describe the average periodic structure, whereas PDF analysis uses suitably reduced total-scattering data to access real-space pair correlations. After appropriate data reduction and Fourier transformation, the pair distribution function, G(r), provides access to local and medium-range structural information that may not be captured by an average crystallographic model. Interpretation still requires structural modelling and awareness of data-reduction and instrumental effects.

Batteries

Electrode local structure

PDF analysis can probe local structural changes associated with cation ordering, nano-phase formation, anion-redox-related structural changes and lithium storage in cathode and anode materials, often in combination with complementary measurements, including operando measurements through charge/discharge cycling.

Catalysis & nanoparticles

Nanoscale structure

For nanostructured and poorly crystalline materials, conventional average-structure models may become insufficient to describe finite-size effects, local distortions and structural disorder. PDF analysis provides real-space pair-correlation information that can complement conventional crystallography, particularly when combined with complementary methods.

Pharmaceuticals

Polymorphs & amorphous content

PDF analysis can support polymorph discrimination and investigations of local order and amorphous components in pharmaceutical formulations prepared via milling, dehydration, compaction or quench cooling.

Disordered materials

A common structural framework

Correlated disorder can leave characteristic signatures in diffraction and total-scattering data. PDF analysis provides a useful structural framework for comparing local correlations across glasses, framework materials, complex oxides, relaxor ferroelectrics and other structurally disordered materials.

Evidence vs. assumption

What the data can support

We distinguish between what the data directly constrain, what requires structural modelling assumptions, and what remains uncertain. The aim is not simply to obtain a good fit, but to identify which structural interpretations are supported by the available evidence.
