Nanoparticles

Sub-10 nm particles broaden and weaken Bragg peaks. Total scattering recovers coordination environments conventional refinement can't reach.

Pharmaceutical molecules

04 — Case

Single crystal

Discrete reflections index directly to a unit cell — the average structure resolved at its most precise.

05 — Case

Amorphous solids

No Bragg peaks at all. PDF is often the only quantitative route to short- and medium-range structure.

Battery electrodes

Cycling drives local disorder that average-structure refinement misses. PDF tracks it directly — pristine vs. cycled.

Data Rescue & Analysis

Give valuable experimental datasets a life.

Every year, researchers collect enormous amounts of data at synchrotron, neutron and laboratory facilities. Time constraints, new projects, staff changes and complex analysis mean not every dataset reaches its full scientific potential — sometimes it just sits on a hard drive. We help researchers revisit previously collected datasets and extract information that may have been overlooked, unexplored or inaccessible with the original analysis.

“The experiment may be finished. The science doesn't have to be.”

Typical situations

A synchrotron experiment was completed but only preliminary analysis was performed.
A neutron scattering dataset was collected but never fully modelled.
A PhD project ended before all measurements could be analysed.
A researcher has inherited datasets from a previous group member.
A publication used only part of a large experimental dataset.
New analysis methods now make an old dataset more informative.
A new scientific question has emerged from an existing dataset.
A facility measurement was technically successful but analysis was postponed.

How we think about it

Beyond Peak Fitting

Many materials contain disorder, nanoscale effects and local distortions that can remain hidden in an average crystallographic model. We combine reciprocal-space and real-space methods to investigate materials across multiple length scales. The goal is not simply to process a dataset, but to answer the scientific question behind it.
